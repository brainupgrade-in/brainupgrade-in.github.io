The problem

AI spend is invisible, uncapped
and mostly wasted

None of these show up on the invoice. The invoice shows only the total.

01 · INPUT

You pay for re-reading

Long chats re-send the whole history every turn. 99% of tokens are input, not output.

02 · LIMITS

Plan limits run out on day 1–2

Pro and Max seats are drained by habits, not by work.

03 · MODEL

The biggest model for every task

Opus to rename a variable. Nobody routes simple prompts to a cheaper model.

04 · SCRIPTS

The LLM does a script's job

Ranking, polling and health checks run through the model, slowly and at full price.

05 · KEYS

Key sprawl, no caps

Personal keys are shared, pasted into repos and leaked. There is no limit per person.

06 · METERS

Nobody can say who spent what

Shared keys and broken counters. We once saw a panel claim 106B tokens a day; the truth was ~13M.

Ask the room: who has run out of their Claude limit this week? Who knows their team's spend per person?
Where the tokens actually go

The cost is what the model reads,
not what it writes

Generation feels expensive because you can watch it. Prefill, which is everything the model reads before it answers, is where the money goes.

Knowledge-discovery tax

Turns spent finding what the team already knows: reading the repo again, re-asking the same questions.

Harness tax

Tools, MCP servers, skills and memory files are injected into every call, whether they are used or not.

Idle tax

Timers and loops that wake the model to say "all fine". 187 of one agent's 346 heartbeat replies were byte-identical.

This is the whole talk in one slide: input dominates, so the levers are about what you send, not what you get back.
The TokenOps method

Three steps, in this order

You cannot cut what you cannot see, and you should not cut before there is a cap. So we measure first, then cap, then cut.

1 · Measure
  • One key per person, team, app or agent
  • Every call logged with tokens and cost
  • A dashboard that matches the vendor invoice
  • A ledger: what was read, and why
2 · Cap
  • Daily budget per key, enforced at the gateway
  • Model allow-list per key
  • Create or revoke a key in seconds
  • No human ever holds a vendor key
3 · Cut
  • Retire turns that do nothing
  • Move mechanical work to scripts
  • Shrink what every call carries
  • Right model per task; caching and batch
Measure → Cap → Cut. The order is the product: capping first makes the cut safe; measuring first makes the cut provable.
